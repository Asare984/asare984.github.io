Week 1

Foundations of Leadership

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

Define leadership and explain the four components that most definitions have in common.
Answer outline
  • Definition: a process in which one person influences a group to reach a common goal (Northouse).
  • Four components: process, influence, group context, goal attainment.
  • Explain each in a line and add one example, such as a course rep organising the class.
With relevant examples, discuss the four levels of leadership.
Answer outline
  • Personal: leading yourself through discipline, integrity and growth.
  • Direct (group): leading a team towards a shared goal.
  • Organisational: influencing the performance of the whole organisation.
  • Strategic: long-term vision, using resources to create value for society.
  • Give one example for each level.
Distinguish between leadership and management and explain why an organisation needs both.
Answer outline
  • Management: doing things right, planning, organising, controlling, short term, accepts the status quo.
  • Leadership: doing the right things, vision, inspiring, long term, challenges the status quo.
  • They overlap. Leadership is part of the directing function of management.
  • Conclude: management brings order, leadership brings direction and change.

Week 2

Theories of Leadership

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

Critically examine Trait Theory, stating its strengths and limitations.
Answer outline
  • Meaning: leaders have certain consistent personal qualities (Stogdill, Kirkpatrick and Locke, Zaccaro).
  • Core traits: intelligence, integrity, sociability, self-confidence, drive and persistence.
  • Strengths: simple, gives a basis for selecting and developing leaders.
  • Limitations: no universal list, subjective, ignores the situation, many people with the traits never lead.
  • Mention the Leadership Trait Questionnaire as a way of measuring traits.
Using the Managerial (Leadership) Grid, explain the five leadership styles and say which you consider most effective.
Answer outline
  • Two dimensions: concern for production and concern for people (Blake and Mouton, 1964).
  • Impoverished (1,1), Country Club (1,9), Task or Authority-Compliance (9,1), Middle-of-the-Road (5,5), Team (9,9).
  • Give the effect on employees and on the organisation for each.
  • Say Team management is most effective and explain why, with an example.
Explain the Pygmalion and Golem effects and how a leader can use them to improve performance.
Answer outline
  • Pygmalion: high expectations lead to higher performance, a self-fulfilling prophecy.
  • Golem: low expectations lead to poor performance and possibly resignation.
  • Examples: a lecturer who believes a student is bright, and a manager who assumes a new staff member is weak.
  • Use: set high but realistic expectations, show belief, give constructive feedback, avoid labels.

Week 3

Contingency Theories of Leadership

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

Explain Fiedler's Contingency Theory, including the LPC scale and the three situational variables.
Answer outline
  • Effectiveness depends on matching leader style with situational favourableness.
  • LPC scale: high LPC (73+) is relationship-oriented, low LPC (54 or below) is task-oriented.
  • Variables: leader-member relations, task structure, position power.
  • Task-oriented leaders do best in very favourable or very unfavourable situations. Relationship-oriented leaders do best in the middle.
  • Limitation: style is assumed to be fixed, so change the situation or the leader.
Using Hersey and Blanchard's Situational Leadership Theory, explain how a leader should adapt style as followers' readiness changes.
Answer outline
  • Readiness means competence and commitment (D1 to D4).
  • S1 Telling, S2 Selling, S3 Participating, S4 Delegating, matched to D1 to D4.
  • Use an example such as the headmaster and four teachers of different experience.
  • Strengths and limitations: practical and simple, but readiness is hard to measure.
Discuss the four leadership styles of Path-Goal Theory and the situations in which each is appropriate.
Answer outline
  • Based on expectancy theory: the leader shows the path to the goal and removes obstacles.
  • Directive, supportive, participative, achievement-oriented.
  • State when to use each (unclear task, stressful work, experienced followers, competent and motivated followers).
  • Factors: follower characteristics and the work environment.
Compare Fiedler's theory with Hersey and Blanchard's theory.
Answer outline
  • Key variable: situation favourableness vs follower readiness.
  • Style: fixed in Fiedler, flexible in Hersey and Blanchard.
  • Measuring tool: LPC scale vs follower development level.
  • Give one strength and one weakness of each.

Week 4

Leadership in Practice and Governance

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

Discuss Kouzes and Posner's five practices of exemplary leadership with relevant examples.
Answer outline
  • Model the way, inspire a shared vision, challenge the process, enable others to act, encourage the heart.
  • One sentence of explanation and one example for each.
  • Conclude that these are learnable behaviours, not personality.
Explain four ways in which effective leadership strengthens governance.
Answer outline
  • Promotes stability: rule of law and peaceful transfer of power.
  • Ensures accountability: audits, transparent reporting.
  • Enhances responsiveness: listening to citizens, adapting policy.
  • Encourages participation: public consultations, inclusive decisions.
  • Optionally add the consequences of poor leadership: corruption, unrest, public distrust.
Discuss the types of organisational culture and the role of leaders in shaping culture.
Answer outline
  • Types: hierarchical, collegial, clan, results-oriented, customer-centric.
  • Leaders shape culture through values they model, what they reward and how they communicate.
  • Culture in turn shapes how leaders can lead.
  • Give an example of each of two cultures.

Week 5

Trust and Power in Leadership

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

Discuss how a leader can build and maintain trust.
Answer outline
  • Define trust: a confident expectation of honesty, dependability and competence, with willingness to be vulnerable.
  • Dimensions: integrity, competence, consistency, loyalty, openness.
  • Building trust: clarity, compassion, character, contribution, competency, connection, commitment, consistency.
  • What destroys trust: self-orientation and lack of transparency.
Explain the seven types of power and distinguish between positional and personal power.
Answer outline
  • Positional: legitimate, reward, coercive.
  • Personal: expert, referent, connection, information.
  • Define each and give a short example.
  • Conclude that personal power lasts longer than positional power.
Why should a leader rely more on reward power than coercive power? Discuss.
Answer outline
  • Both influence behaviour, but coercion works through fear.
  • Fear damages trust and morale and raises resistance and turnover.
  • Rewards build commitment and motivation.
  • Link to trust and personal power as the more lasting sources of influence.

Week 6

Traditional Leadership

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

Explain the pre-colonial systems of traditional leadership in Ghana and how colonial rule affected them.
Answer outline
  • Two systems: centralised (king or paramount chief at the apex) and decentralised (no single ruler).
  • Colonial rule used chiefs for indirect rule and added a judicial role.
  • Effect: blamed for some modern chieftaincy conflicts.
Discuss the recognition and organisation of chieftaincy under the 1992 Constitution.
Answer outline
  • Article 270: recognises chieftaincy, and Parliament cannot take away or give a chief's recognition.
  • Article 271: National House of Chiefs. Article 274: Regional Houses.
  • Article 273: judicial committee and appeals.
  • Article 275: disqualification and the bar on active party politics.
  • Mention the Chieftaincy Act 1971 (Act 370) and the ministry.
Discuss the roles of traditional leaders in modern Ghana and whether chieftaincy still has a place in a democracy.
Answer outline
  • Roles: political, social, military in the past and judicial under colonial rule, now development partner and custodian of land and custom.
  • Arguments against: conflicts with democracy, cost, disputes.
  • Arguments for: legitimacy, community mobilisation, culture.
  • Give a balanced conclusion.

Week 7

Leadership Styles and Skills

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

With relevant examples, discuss the critical roles interpersonal and communication skills play in the leader-member relationship. From your lecture slides
Answer outline
  • Interpersonal: coaching, sensitivity, handling conflict discreetly, understanding people's needs.
  • Communication: oral, written and non-verbal. Clear communication avoids confusion and builds trust.
  • Show how these shape the relationship: trust, motivation, fewer conflicts, better performance.
  • Give a specific example for each skill.
Identify any two intrapersonal skills you need as a leader and explain how they help you lead effectively. From your lecture slides
Answer outline
  • Pick two, such as self-awareness, self-discipline, emotional control, self-motivation or integrity. The slides ask the question but do not list the skills, so define your chosen skills clearly.
  • For each: meaning, how it affects decisions and behaviour, and an example.
  • Link to leading yourself before leading others.
Compare transformational and transactional leadership.
Answer outline
  • Transformational: inspires beyond self-interest. Bass's four I's: idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individual consideration.
  • Transactional: exchange of reward for performance: contingent reward, management by exception (active and passive).
  • Compare focus, motivation and best use. Give an example of each.
Explain Lewin's three leadership styles and when each is appropriate.
Answer outline
  • Authoritarian: leader decides alone. Use when time is short or the leader knows most.
  • Participative: leader guides and invites input. Found to be the most effective.
  • Laissez-faire: little direction. Works only with highly skilled, self-driven people.
  • Give an example for each.

Week 9

Decision-Making Models and Styles

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

Explain the Cognitive Resource Model and say when a leader's intelligence or experience matters.
Answer outline
  • Fiedler and Garcia (1987).
  • Low stress: intelligence helps. High stress: experience matters more.
  • Intelligence helps the team only when the leader is directive.
  • Strength: stresses leader capacity and stress management. Weakness: terms such as intelligence and stress are not clearly defined.
Discuss Vroom and Yetton's five decision strategies and the factors a leader considers in choosing one.
Answer outline
  • A1, A2 (autocratic), C1, C2 (consultative), G2 (group).
  • Good decisions need quality and acceptance, within the time available.
  • Use the diagnostic questions: is quality critical, is acceptance critical, do followers share the organisation's goals?
  • Give an example for two of the strategies.
Distinguish between rational and incremental decision-making and state when each is appropriate.
Answer outline
  • Rational: identify the problem, list alternatives, weigh cost against benefit, choose the best. Needs time and information.
  • Incremental (Lindblom): small adjustments to existing policy. Used when time, information or money is limited.
  • Give an example of each, such as public policy for incremental.

Week 10

Leading Teams, Groups and Crisis

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

Discuss five pitfalls your team would have to be mindful of if you are to succeed in a given assignment. Use relevant examples to support your points. From your lecture slides
Answer outline
  • Choose five from: unrealistic expectations, conflict over work styles, focus on results only, resistance to change, poor team skills, lack of trust, social loafing, groupthink, vague assignments.
  • For each: explain the pitfall, its effect on the team and how to avoid it.
  • Use group-assignment examples from your own class.
Explain Tuckman's five stages of group development and the leader's role at each stage.
Answer outline
  • Forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning.
  • What happens at each stage.
  • Leader's role: clarify direction in forming, manage conflict in storming, set norms in norming, delegate in performing, give recognition in adjourning.
Distinguish between a group and a team, and discuss social loafing and groupthink and how a leader can reduce them.
Answer outline
  • Group: people who interact and share goals. Team: complementary skills, mutual accountability, shared purpose.
  • Social loafing: less effort in a group than alone. Reduce by making contributions identifiable, keeping groups small, rewarding group success.
  • Groupthink: pressure for harmony leads to poor decisions. Reduce by inviting disagreement and different views.
Define a crisis and discuss the demands it places on a leader.
Answer outline
  • Definition: a specific, unexpected, non-routine event creating high uncertainty and threat to top organisational goals (Seeger et al., 1998).
  • A crisis tests a leader's real substance.
  • Apply the team-leading principles: clear communication, trust, quick decisions, keeping the team together.
  • Use an example, such as a flood or a sudden loss of funds.

Week 11

Ethical Leadership and Values

Try each question first, then tap it to check the answer outline.

Discuss Northouse's five principles of ethical leadership with relevant examples.
Answer outline
  • Respect others, serve others, be just, be honest, build community.
  • Explain each principle in a line and give an example (for instance mentoring and coaching for serving others).
  • Conclude that ethical leaders both decide and lead ethically.
You are a judge in a robbery trial involving your only child. Discuss the ethical dilemma and how an ethical leader should act. From your lecture slides
Answer outline
  • Define an ethical dilemma: a choice between equally unfavourable options.
  • Conflict: loyalty to family against justice and fairness.
  • Apply the principles: be just, be honest, respect others.
  • Practical steps: declare the conflict of interest and step aside. Link to the 1992 Constitution's warning on conflict of interest.
Distinguish between ethics, morality and values, and explain the institutions that promote accountability in Ghana.
Answer outline
  • Ethics: principles for judging right and wrong. Morality: the rules of conduct. Values: what a group judges to be right.
  • Shared values are the bedrock of trust between leader and followers.
  • Institutions: CHRAJ, EOCO, Economic Crime Court, Public Accounts Committee, Auditor-General, Office of Accountability, Special Prosecutor.
  • Explain what two of them do.
